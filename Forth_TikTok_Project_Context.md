## 1. What this project is

A TikTok series fronted by Yelden Sarybay for a **UK audience**, explaining how international trade actually works: **who does what**, **which platforms are used**, **the nuances that catch people out**, and **how tariffs end up in the price a shopper pays**.

**Business goal:** the series exists to bring **enquiries to Forth Import Export** (sourcing and import management) and, secondarily, readers to Forth Trading Intelligence. Creator Rewards income is a bonus, not a target (section 5).

**Primary audience:** small UK online sellers and would-be importers. **Reach audience:** curious UK shoppers. See section 3 for the full audience matrix.

**Format:** mainly talking-head videos, with inserts of graphs, company logos, news headlines and on-screen definitions.

**Spine:** real stories. Each video is built around a real case from relatively recent news, summarised as a case study, and the trade process is explained through that story rather than in the abstract.

## 5. TikTok facts that shape the format

- **Video length:** uploads can run up to 60 minutes; recording in the TikTok app is limited to 10 minutes.
- **Creator Rewards Program:** only pays for videos at least **one minute** long. Eligibility: 18 or over, 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in the last 30 days. These are the US programme terms; check the UK terms in TikTok Studio when eligible. **Secondary to enquiries.**
- **How distribution works:** each video is shown to a small batch of strangers first and widened only if they stay. Episodes 1 to 3 each spiked for a few hours and flattened when the first batch swiped away.
- **Retention is everything:** average watch time and completion rate drive reach. The number to watch is retention at 0:03 (target above 60%), then where the curve drops next.
- **Search value** counts, so say the key terms out loud and put them in captions. Episode 1 was found by a search for "china tariffs"; search rose to 3.2% of Episode 3's traffic.
- **Analytics:** per-video analysis in the app, and the TikTok Studio app for more. Traffic sources and audience data appear once a video passes 100 views. The "Inspiration" tab's trending and similar posts are generic and not useful for this niche.
- **Bio:** the field takes at least 150 characters on this account.

## 6. Best practices learned so far

The full set, with the reason for each, is in Forth_TikTok_Rules.docx. The essentials:

- **Sanity-check the premise** before deep research: stories move on.
- **Two passes, then primary sources**, with a fact-check file giving every claim a status: Confirmed, Corrected, Not published or Could not access.
- **No primary source, no figure.** Check exactly what a figure counts and what a label covers. Keep claims as cautious as the evidence.
- **Separate who pays a tariff legally from who bears the cost.**
- **Re-check dates in this document before filming.**
- **Writing:** complete sentences, 260 to 280 words, one cut-first sentence, British English (aeroplane, not airplane), the right currency for every figure.
- **Editing:** check the take against the script; trim pauses rather than speeding up; inserts as cutaways; text inside the safe area; fix subtitles.
- **Ideas for topics** beyond the news: TikTok search autocomplete, Google Trends (UK), Reddit seller communities, MoneySavingExpert, trade press, chamber and trade-body surveys, the sourcing calendar (Canton Fair, Chinese New Year), platform rule changes, and Forth Intel data.

## 8. Episode 1: the thesis video

**Status:** posted 2 November 2026 on a brand-new account. Four takes cut together.

**Story in one line:** in April 2025 everyone predicted Chinese goods blocked by US tariffs would flood Britain; Yelden tested it for his thesis and couldn't find a clear jump.

### Accuracy guardrails

- Say **"no clear jump"** or "I couldn't find it", never "there was no diversion". The thesis found no statistically significant average effect, and leaves room for slower, sector-level adjustment.
- The 145% US tariff and the Currys boss's "straightforward dumping" warning were both reported in mid-April 2025.
- **Don't put a single import figure on screen.** Press reports said UK imports from China rose 11% year on year in April 2025, but HMRC's own April release shows £4.5bn, down 12.4% on March. If challenged: overall imports moving is not the same as the tariffed goods pulling ahead.
- The fridge point is an **illustration** taken from a retailer's comment at the time (Buy It Direct's Nick Glynne), not a thesis finding.

### Thesis facts for answering comments

- **Data:** monthly UK imports from China, January 2020 to 2025, from UN Comtrade and the ONS. Products grouped at HS4 level into tariff-exposed and non-exposed.
- **Methods:** interrupted time series on total imports; difference-in-differences comparing exposed and non-exposed products; an event study around April 2025.
- **Results:** no statistically significant break in total imports at April 2025 once trend and seasonality are accounted for. The difference-in-differences estimate is positive but not significant (beta = 0.0589, SE 0.0584, p = 0.314). No sign of diverging trends beforehand (joint pre-trend test p = 0.171). Conclusions hold with February, April and May 2025 as the start date.
- **Interpretation in the thesis:** adjustment is more likely staged, sector-specific and filtered through firms' caution (buffering with inventory, waiting, diversifying gradually) than a single clean break.

**Status:** posted 3 November 2026. Full script, insert plan, verified facts and "do not use" list in the 3 November version of this document and in ep2_factcheck.md.

**Story in one line:** Shein and Temu grew on a rule that lets small parcels in without customs duty; America and Europe have closed it, Britain hasn't yet, and HMRC's figures show no clear jump in Britain after the others closed.

### Do not use (Episode 2 topics)

- "£3bn of parcels from China, up from £1.3bn": no official source.
- "64%" (EU fall) and "93%" (China share): use "more than 60%" and "over 90%".
- "1.6 million parcels a day": HMRC's figures are consignments, not parcels.
- The £5.9bn BIRDS figure next to the HMRC low-value series: different measures and periods.
- Temu UK's $171m revenue as UK sales: it is the UK entity's revenue.

### Answering comments (Episode 2)

- **"Shein did pay duty."** The video only says nothing on their small parcels, which is how the committee's table labels it.
- **"So there was no flood?"** "No clear jump in HMRC's figures so far", never "no diversion".
- **"Is it tax free?"** No: VAT is charged at checkout. Only customs duty is relieved.

### Episode 3: Scotch whisky and the Airbus fight

**Status:** posted 4 November 2026, 83 seconds. Hook on screen: "Why was Scotch punished for a plane?" Ended without naming the next episode.

**Lessons:** the hook worked; the backstory came too early (section 7). For future episodes, reorder as hook, consequence, then "here's how". Say "aeroplane", not "airplane".

### Confirmed (premise check)

- WTO authorised US$7.5bn of countermeasures on 14 October 2019; the 25% tariff on single malt Scotch, Northern Irish single malt, liqueurs and some wines took effect on 18 October 2019.
- Blended Scotch was spared. Britain was one of the four Airbus countries, so the honest framing is that the UK helped fund Airbus and whisky makers picked up the bill.
- US importers paid the tariff at the border; distillers paid through lost sales. Use the SWA's own figures for export losses, not the £500m or "over £1bn" in other coverage.
- Suspended from March 2021, then for five years from July 2021; single malt goes into the US tariff-free from 24 July 2026, while the separate 10% on other UK goods remains. The US warned in October 2026 that an EU loan to Airbus threatens the US-EU truce.

## 12. How to write the script

*Added 5 November 2026, from the lessons of Episodes 1 to 3. The beat timings are in section 13; the reasons behind each rule are in Forth_TikTok_Rules.docx.*

### Before writing a word

- **The fact-check file is finished.** Every figure is Confirmed or Corrected, with its primary source. A figure that is Not published or Could not access stays out of the script.
- **Write one sentence that answers "so what?" for the target segment.** For a seller story: what should a seller do differently after watching? If there is no answer, the story is for reach, and the mix should allow for that.
- **Find the conflict:** the money lost, the person caught out, or the cost nobody saw coming. That becomes the hook.
- **Find the twist:** the fact most viewers won't expect. Hold it back for the middle of the video.

### The opening: the first six seconds decide everything

- **Sentence 1, the hook (0 to 3 seconds):** the first five or six spoken words carry the conflict. No date, no place name, no "so" or "today" first.
- **Sentence 2, the consequence (by about 0:06):** deliver what the hook promised, straight away. Episode 3 lost most viewers at 0:04 because 16 seconds of backstory came before the tariff.
- **Open a loop before the backstory:** tell the viewer what is coming and that it will be explained, for example "and the reason comes down to one line in a contract, which I'll get to". It gives a reason to stay past 0:04. Always pay the loop off, and never promise a cause the fact-check file doesn't support.
- **Only then the backstory,** introduced with a bridge such as "and to understand why, you have to...". The viewer now knows where it is going, so they will sit through it.
- **The on-screen text says the hook in fewer words,** as a question with stakes, shown while you say it.

**Worked example, Episode 3 reordered:**

> Scotch whisky was punished for Britain's loans to Airbus,
> even though no distillery has ever built an aeroplane.
> In 2019, America put a 25% tariff on single malt Scotch,
> and to understand why, you have to follow the money
> that Britain, France, Germany and Spain lent to Airbus...

Weak openings to avoid: "In April 2025, Britain was told..." (a date first); "Today I want to talk about..." (no conflict); "Have you ever wondered..." (a question with no stakes).

### The middle

- **The cast:** name each role by what it does in this story, not by its job title alone. "The customs broker, the person who fills in the import forms" works better than "the customs broker" on its own.
- **The twist:** delivered still and straight to camera, in one sentence.
- **The why:** one mechanism and one concrete example. If a second example is tempting, it is usually the cut-first sentence.
- **Separate who pays from who bears the cost** whenever a tariff, fee or fine is involved.
- **Mark one cut-first sentence** with [CUT FIRST], so the edit can lose it without a reshoot.
- **Say the search terms aloud:** tariff, customs, import, Amazon, Etsy, HMRC, and the product itself.

### The ending

- **Takeaway:** one line the viewer can use, aimed at the target segment. For sellers, something they can check or do; for shoppers, something they will notice next time they buy.
- **Then one of:** the name of the next episode (only if it is certain), a two-option question ("Next, the tomato shortage or the Porsches stuck at port? Tell me in the comments."), or nothing.
- **Then the sign-off**, word for word, every time: "I'm Yelden. Follow for more real stories about how trade works."

### Sentences and words

- **Complete, flowing sentences** joined with while, and, which, because: the way Yelden speaks. No fragments or one-word lines.
- **Teleprompter layout:** one line per breath, about 8 to 12 words, but each sentence runs on to its full stop.
- **Length:** 260 to 280 words, which lands at 75 to 100 seconds and always over one minute.
- **British English:** aeroplane, tyre, jumper, programme. Pounds where possible, but the right currency for each figure, said in full ("seventy million dollars").
- **Numbers:** round them the way the source allows ("more than 60%", not "64%" when the source says more than 60%). One number per sentence at most.
- **Caution words where the evidence is thin:** "no clear jump", "so far", "the industry says". Never stronger than the fact-check file.
- **People and companies:** no private individuals named; a company only where the primary source names it; a seller or importer that a notice doesn't name stays unnamed.
- **No em dashes and no diacritics.**

### Script file layout

Each script is written in this layout, so it can go straight into the teleprompter and the edit:

```
[HOOK | on screen: "Why was Scotch punished for a plane?" | insert: plane and whisky glass]
Line one of the hook,
line two of the hook.

[CONSEQUENCE | insert: 25% number card]
...

[CUT FIRST] The sentence that can go.

[TAKEAWAY | back to face]

[SIGN-OFF]

Sources: one line per figure, matching the fact-check file.
```

### Variant: business-model episodes

For episodes on how a company makes money (Global-e, Alibaba, Amazon's marketplace, inspection firms, forwarders):

- **Hook:** the invisible middleman, or a news peg such as a recall or a ruling that involves the company.
- **Consequence:** what that means for a seller who uses them.
- **How the money flows:** who pays them and for what: fees, commission, currency margin, subscriptions, advertising.
- **The twist:** the part of the model most people miss, such as a free service paid for elsewhere.
- **Takeaway:** what a seller should check before using them.
- **Sources:** the company's own annual report, prospectus or filings. Explain, don't review: no endorsement and no claim the filings don't support.

### Before filming: script check

- Read it aloud once with a timer: 75 to 100 seconds?
- Does the conflict land in the first six words, and the consequence by sentence two?
- Does every figure match the fact-check file, in the right currency?
- Is the takeaway useful to the target segment?
- Re-check dates and figures on the day: rules and deadlines move.

## 13. Video format and production

| Beat | Time | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 3 s | First words carry the conflict; on-screen question inside the safe area; picture changes by 0:02. |
| Consequence | to ~6 s | What happened as a result, in one sentence. The payoff the hook promised. |
| Here's how | to ~25 s | Backstory, only now, with a headline or graphic. |
| The cast | to ~45 s | Who was involved and what each does (definition cards). |
| The twist | to ~65 s | The surprising turn, still and straight to camera. |
| Why | to ~85 s | The mechanism, with one concrete example. |
| Takeaway | last 10 s | One usable line, then the sign-off; optionally the next episode or a two-option question. |

- **Filming:** recent episodes were filmed indoors at a desk. Face the light, test sound on earphones, look into the lens, one take per section, CapCut teleprompter with a practice read to set the speed.
- **Zoom cuts:** punch in about 10% on a punchline or a new point, then cut back to the normal frame. The cheapest way to change the picture between inserts.
- **Story props:** where possible, open with a real object from the story in shot or in hand (the whisky glass, a charger, a parcel, a plug). Not generic toys like a model container, which undercut credibility.
- **Inserts:** something changes every 3 to 5 seconds; graphs carry one message with units and source; headlines show outlet and date; definition cards in one recognisable design.
- **Safe area:** about 150px from the top, 450px from the bottom, 140px from the right and 60px from the left of a 1080x1920 frame.
- **Voice:** British English, pounds where possible, the right currency for each figure, no em dashes, no diacritics.

## 14. Accuracy and sourcing rules

- Every story is checked against reliable sources **before** it is scripted; figures and dates come from primary sources, never from memory.
- Each episode has a fact-check file (claim, verified figure, primary source, status) before filming, and a short source list for the caption or a pinned comment.
- Claims stay as cautious as the evidence: "no clear jump", not "it didn't happen".
- Forth Intel numbers only from the verified report data; any UK import figure shown under the Forth name is checked against the report canon.
- No stories from Forth's own clients; no private individuals named. Illustrations are flagged as illustrations.
