## B. Definitions (question 1)

| # | Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | A duty suspension removes or reduces import duty on specific goods, "normally those used in production processes, for a defined period" | Confirmed | Guidance, as A1 |
| B2 | Suspensions allow **unlimited** quantities in at the reduced rate; autonomous tariff quotas (ATQs) allow only **limited** quantities | Confirmed | Guidance |
| B3 | Suspensions apply on a Most Favoured Nation basis: goods in scope can come from any country at the reduced rate, subject to any conditions | Confirmed | Guidance |
| B4 | Suspensions are temporary and "can be used by any UK or Crown Dependency importer while in force" | Confirmed | Guidance |
| B5 | They do not cut VAT or trade remedy duties such as anti-dumping duty | Confirmed | Guidance; government response |
| B6 | Contrast with a trade deal preference: a preference cuts duty only for goods that originate in a partner country and meet its rules of origin. A suspension applies whatever the origin (B3). The origin rule is general knowledge and is not stated on the suspension page, so keep the card wording general | Confirmed for the suspension side; preference side from general customs knowledge | Guidance, as A1 |

**Definition card (plain English):**
> **Duty suspension:** the government sets the import duty on a product to zero, or lower, for a fixed period. It applies to every importer, from any country, in unlimited amounts. It does not cut VAT.
>
> Compared with: a **tariff quota** (a lower rate, but only for a limited amount) and a **trade deal preference** (a lower rate, but only for goods made in a partner country).

**Cautious answer to question 3:** any UK importer can *use* a suspension once it is in force. On the 2024 rules nothing barred a small business or sole trader from *applying*. But the scheme is built for production inputs, an applicant must show there is no adequate UK production, and repacking or simple assembly does not count as production. A seller importing finished consumer goods for resale is therefore not the typical applicant and would need to make a special case. Do not say that sellers can get their duty cut. Say that they can ask, and that most successful requests are for materials that UK factories use.

## F. How it works on the declaration and the tariff (questions 5 and 6)

| # | Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | On the UK Trade Tariff a suspension appears as measure type **112 "Autonomous tariff suspension"**, or **115 "Autonomous suspension under authorised use"** when there is an end-use condition. The legal base is SI 2020/1435 | Confirmed | Trade tariff API, commodities 0409000010 and 2309903199 |
| F2 | To claim a normal business suspension, the declaration uses document code **9Y12** with the statement "Normal business suspensions". ## G. Catches, traps and twists (question 8)

| # | Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Twist: you do the work and everyone benefits.** A suspension is open to any UK importer, including the applicant's competitors | Confirmed | B4; D11 |
| G2 | Your request is made public: the list of applications is published for anyone to object, including UK producers | Confirmed (2024 rules; the 2026 notice was seen in search results but not opened) | D4 page 10 |

| G4 | It does not touch VAT, and it does not override anti-dumping duty | Confirmed | B5 |

## 2. Suggested five beats (75 to 100 seconds)

1. **Hook (0 to 6 s):** one of the hooks above with its consequence line, the open loop by about 0:08, and a real tin of baked beans or a jar of Manuka honey in hand (R22).
2. **The story (6 to 30 s):** from 26 November tariffs fall to zero on 101 food products, kerosene and fertiliser. This was government-led, but alongside it about 269 suspensions that businesses asked for have been extended to the end of 2028, and a new window runs from 11 November to 3 February.
3. **How it works (30 to 60 s):** you name the product and its commodity code, and show there is little or no UK production and a real saving. The product is published so that others can object, the government assesses it, and HM Treasury changes the law. In the last round this took about 8 months, and it shows on the tariff as a 0% suspension. Your customs broker, the person who fills in the import forms, claims it with code 9Y12.
4. **The catch (60 to 80 s), still, to camera:** it is not yours alone, because every importer of that product gets it, including your rivals. It is built for materials that factories use, while repacking finished goods does not count as production. It expires, and it does not touch VAT.
   [CUT FIRST] "It also only covers the exact product description, so a near-miss on the code means full duty."
5. **Takeaway and sign-off (last 10 to 15 s):** "Before the third of February, look up your product's code on the UK Trade Tariff, and if you pay duty on something nobody in Britain makes, it may be worth asking." Then: "I'm Yelden. Follow for more real stories about how trade works."

## 4. Open questions and risks

1. **This year's criteria are not published until 11 November.** Re-check section D, and especially whether finished goods, a minimum saving or applicant type are addressed, before scripting the "how it works" beat. Take a screenshot then.
2. **Manuka honey (D12, F3):** confirmed as a business suspension of a finished food product, but the applicant and the reasoning are not published. Use it only as "on the list", not as "a seller won this".
3. **Who decides:** no single named decision-maker is published. Say "the government" on screen, and if needed, "the Department for Business and Trade runs it and HM Treasury changes the law". Check the department's current name (H).
4. **The baked beans tariff page will change on 26 November.** Any "before" screenshot (16%) must be taken before then, and the "after" (0%) checked from 26 November.
5. **Filming date:** if posting before 26 November, the food cut is future tense. If after 3 February 2027, the window has closed and the takeaway must change (R27).
6. **"Almost nobody does" (hook 1)** has no published count of applicants versus importers. Soften it to "most small importers have never heard of it" (an opinion, flag as illustration under R34), or use "over 400 suspensions have been granted at businesses' request since Brexit" (E8).

8. **Not opened:** the published notice of 2025 to 2026 applications (it would show what kinds of firms and products apply) and the full Budget Table 6.1 (business suspension cost figures). Both are worth reading if a comment asks "who actually applies?"
